    Skill version: 0.1 Student: elena Model: sonnet-4-6 Date: 2026-06-13

    AI: Here is a question fashion buyers face all the time:

    A cotton T-shirt and a polyester T-shirt are sitting on a desk in front of you. A colleague says the cotton one is more sustainable because it is natural. Another colleague says the polyester one is more sustainable because it lasts longer. Who is right — and how would you actually find out?

    Student: it depends on how long you wear it

    AI: Exactly — and that instinct to ask "depends on what?" is the beginning of the LCA mindset. You have just identified something that trips up a lot of sustainability marketing: a claim like "this fabric is eco-friendly" is meaningless unless you say compared to what, measured how, and over how long.

    This is precisely the problem that LCA — Life Cycle Assessment was invented to solve. LCA is a method for adding up all the environmental impacts of making, using, and disposing of a product by tracing every step in its supply chain. Think of it like a receipt that shows every hidden cost of a product — not in money, but in CO₂ emissions, water use, and chemical pollution. It forces you to be specific about exactly what you are measuring and over what period of time, so that comparisons are actually fair.

    For someone working in fashion sourcing or buying, this matters in a very practical way: when a supplier hands you a sustainability certificate, LCA is the method behind it. Without LCA, "sustainable" is just a word on a hangtag. With it, you can ask: sustainable compared to what, measured how, and over which part of the product's life?

    Ready to see how a real LCA study is structured — what the four steps look like from start to finish?

    Student: no i want some more examples

    AI: Of course! Here are a few more situations where "it depends" is exactly the right answer — and where LCA is the tool that helps you find out what it depends on.

    A tote bag vs a plastic bag

    A cotton tote bag takes a lot of energy and water to produce — far more than a single plastic bag. Studies have shown you need to use a cotton tote around 150 times before its carbon footprint per use drops below a disposable plastic bag's. So is the tote more sustainable? It depends entirely on how many times you actually use it.

    Organic cotton vs conventional cotton

    Organic cotton avoids synthetic pesticides, which sounds like a clear win. But organic farming typically produces less cotton per acre, which means more land is needed to grow the same amount of fibre. More land can mean more water use and more habitat disruption. Is organic better? It depends on which environmental problem you care most about — chemical pollution, water, or land use.

    A wool jumper vs a polyester fleece

    Wool is natural and biodegradable. But sheep release methane during digestion, and methane is a very potent greenhouse gas — far more warming than CO₂ kilogram for kilogram. A polyester fleece has no methane problem, but it is made from fossil fuels and sheds microplastics when washed. Which is worse? It depends on whether you are more concerned about climate change, fossil fuel use, or ocean pollution.

    In every case, LCA is the tool that replaces "it depends" with an actual number — so you can make the comparison honestly.
