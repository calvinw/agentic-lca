**AI:** Here is why this matters for someone working in fashion or retail.

When a supplier sends you a sustainability certificate, LCA is the method behind it. Without LCA, "sustainable" is just a word on a hangtag — with it, you can ask: sustainable compared to what, measured how, and over which part of the product's life?

LCA also tells you where the biggest environmental impacts actually are. Maybe 80% of a shirt's carbon footprint comes from the fabric mill, not the cotton farm — that changes where a designer or buyer should focus their efforts.

---

Here is the map of how an LCA study works — four phases. Think of it like a recipe with four steps:

1. **Goal and scope** — Decide why you are doing the study and draw a fence around what you are including. Without this step, the results cannot be compared fairly to anything else.

2. **Inventory** — Collect the data. How much cotton? How much electricity? How much water? A big spreadsheet for every step inside the fence.

3. **Impact assessment** — Convert the raw data into environmental scores. Turn kilograms of CO₂ and methane into a single climate-change number, so you can see the big picture.

4. **Interpretation** — Read the results and decide what to do. Where is the impact coming from? What can you change?

Which of those four phases do you think would be the hardest to do in practice — and why?

**Student:** 3

**AI:** Good pick — and you are not alone in that. Phase 3 trips a lot of people up at first.

Here is why it feels hard: the inventory gives you a big list of different emissions — CO₂, methane, ammonia, sulfur dioxide — all measured in kilograms. But you cannot just add them up, because a kilogram of methane warms the planet about 28 times more than a kilogram of CO₂. It is like adding up amounts in different currencies — euros, yen, pounds — without knowing the exchange rates.

**Impact assessment is the currency exchange.** It uses published conversion numbers (called characterization factors) to translate every emission into a common unit — like "kg of CO₂ equivalent" — so you can add them together and see the total climate impact.

That is the concept. In the next skill (impact characterization), you actually do the maths by hand with real data. But first — every LCA study has to start with the first phase: **goal and scope**, deciding why you are doing the study and what you include.
